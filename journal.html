Day 01July 2026
Campaign live

It’s live. Here’s what that actually means.

The Kickstarter is up. After months of scripts, scouting, and buying fake blood in quantities that raise eyebrows at the till — there’s a page with a button on it and a number that starts at zero.

Let’s be straight about what this is. We’re making this film either way. Our own money is already in it. The Kickstarter doesn’t decide whether Echoes of Crimson exists — it decides how far it travels, how much we can pay the people who turn up, and whether the version you eventually watch looks like what’s in our heads or a compromise we settled for.

On backing it

Tiers start at £5. From £25 you get both cuts — Eileen’s chaos comedy horror and Jason’s darker, colder one, the same footage taken into two rooms and coming out as two different films. Above that there’s signed artwork, DVDs, screen‑worn costume, and a handful of one‑of‑one props that when they’re gone are gone.

If you’re going to back it, back it early. We’re not saying that to rush you. Kickstarter’s algorithm surfaces campaigns that move fast in the first couple of days, and a project that stalls out of the gate rarely recovers no matter how good it is. A fiver on day one genuinely does more work than fifty quid in week three. That’s not sales talk, it’s just how the platform behaves.

And every backer name goes up on the Backer Wall, in the order it arrived. First in, top of the wall, and it stays that way for good.

On not backing it

Here’s the part we mean most. Not everyone has spare money, and we’re not going to pretend otherwise. Times are hard and a horror film is not anybody’s priority over a food shop. If you can’t pledge, that is completely fine, and you are not less welcome here for it.

Sharing is real support. It costs nothing and it works. Independent films don’t find their audience through advertising budgets — they find it because somebody tagged a mate who likes British horror. Post the link. Repost the poster. Tell one person who’d get it. Leave a comment on the campaign, because comments push a project up the page in a way that silence never will.

We have watched films we love disappear because nobody outside the room ever heard about them. If you spread this thing further than it would have gone on its own, you have done something for it. We’ll take that as gladly as a pledge, and we mean that plainly.

Where to start

If you want to know who’s making this before you decide anything — fair enough. The Watch page now has Eileen’s showreel, Jason’s showreel, and trailers for what the pair of us have made before. Thirty years of British horror between us and no interest in slowing down.

Then come back and press the button. Or don’t, and tell somebody instead. Both help.

Day 00July 2026
Pre‑production

Before a single frame

Nothing's been shot yet. We're writing this from the wrong side of it — scripts locked, locations scouted, fake blood ordered in a quantity that got a look at the till. August is when the cameras roll. This is us telling you before, not after.

The experiment: one shoot, one cast, one set of performances in the fog and cobbles of 1888 Whitechapel. Then the footage goes into two rooms and comes out as two films. Eileen cuts the comedy horror — chaos, teeth, jokes that shouldn't land and do. Jason cuts the dark one — slower, colder, the version that follows you home. Neither is the "real" cut. That's the point.

We're making it either way. Our own money's already down and we're not waiting for anyone's blessing. The Kickstarter decides how far it travels, nothing more. If you're going to back it, back it early — early is the only thing that actually moves the needle.
