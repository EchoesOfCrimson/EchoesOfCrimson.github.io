Day 03August 2026
On set · 7 days to go

We’re on set. It’s happening.

It’s really happening.

We are on set today. Cameras are up, it’s time to start rolling. We are finally on set today, planning the shots, planning the scenes, making this happen!

Keith, and a book full of ghosts

Before anything else, this. Keith brought his copy of The Jack the Ripper A‑Z to set — the one he co‑authored — and opened it for us.

Twenty‑six years later the same man is standing in our set, making sure we get 1888 right. That’s not a coincidence we planned. It’s just what happens when you ask the right person to keep you honest.

The day itself

Set dressing, wardrobe, props, and a great deal of standing about deciding where a chair goes. This is the part nobody puts in the trailer.

Ravens, tarot, a wall of books nobody should read after dark. Somebody pressing costumes, because somebody always has to. Eileen mid‑brew and mid‑plan. And the skull sharing a table with a Mademoiselle disc — one for the film, one for the backer rewards.

Your support and pledges have been wonderful. We thank each and every one of you for sharing, posting, liking and contributing.

What’s happening now

There are seven days left on the Kickstarter. One week. Then it’s closed. Now is the time if you want to be part of our epic journey — share, pledge, support.

So, while we’re covered in fake blood, three small favours:

  • Share the campaign with someone who has questionable taste in films
  • Follow our new Facebook page — set photos are landing there as we shoot
  • Pledge or pledge up while there’s still time

Everything extra goes straight onto the screen. Hard drives remain outrageously expensive, and the cast’s taste in catering has not improved.

Thank you for getting us here. Back to work — there’s a murder to film!

See you in the fog,
The Echoes of Crimson Team

Day 02August 2026
185% funded

One hundred and eighty‑five per cent

We said if we scraped the target we’d be happy. We’re at 185% and we’ve had to sit down about it more than once.

So here’s what that actually buys, because it isn’t a number on a page. Every penny of it goes into the film. All of it. This is the difference between indie fun and certifiable chaos.

More blood. More props. Better wardrobe. Better sets. Better food on set — and we’re only half joking about that one, because fed actors are happy actors and happy actors do the work that ends up on screen. Anyone who’s spent a cold twelve hours on a no‑budget shoot knows exactly how much a proper hot meal is worth, and it isn’t a perk.

Then there’s the part nobody puts on a poster: the edit, and somewhere safe to keep the footage while two people take it apart in two different directions. Unglamorous, expensive, and the difference between a film that gets finished properly and one that just gets finished.

Everything gets better the further this goes. That’s not a sales line, it’s arithmetic.

Two new rewards

The first one has a story attached, so bear with us.

Mademoiselle is Tony Richardson’s 1966 film, written by Jean Genet, with Jeanne Moreau doing something genuinely unsettling in the lead. The BFI put it out as a dual format edition. It’s a proper piece of British film history and it has nothing to do with Whitechapel.

Except that it does. Turn the case over.

That’s our Keith. He was in it, sixty years ago. He’s the one now keeping us honest on the 1888 material — the same Keith Skinner who consulted on From Hell and Ripper Street, and who has spent a good part of his life making sure the Whitechapel murders are treated as something that happened to five real women rather than a plot device.

We’ve got copies, and Keith is signing them. A film he acted in as a young man, signed by the man who now stops us getting the history wrong. There aren’t many and there won’t be more.

The second one is a new perfume made for this film, joining the rest of the range — Mr Crispin, Witches Brew, She’s a Bitch, Hollywood Betrayed, First Bite. Signed by Eileen. We’re not telling you what’s in it yet.

More rewards coming as we go. Some of these are things we’ve wanted to make for years and finally have an excuse to.

Where we are right now

The score. Eileen and Ben are working out what this film sounds like, and they have opinions. Different ones. It’s coming out better for it.

Props, sets, and the shooting plan. We are currently surrounded by things we have bought for this film and cannot explain to anyone who walks in.

End of August. We shoot. That’s when this stops being posters and starts being a film.

After that. Two edits. Eileen’s and Jason’s. Same footage, two entirely different animals.

Thank you, and keep going

To everyone who’s backed us, shared us, or just quietly kept an eye on this — thank you. We are so grateful it’s slightly embarrassing. We don’t have a marketing budget or a distributor or anyone’s permission. We have you, and it turns out that’s been enough.

At this point the share button does more for us than the pledge button. Every person who passes this to someone who likes horror is doing the job of an advertising spend we haven’t got. Keep sharing. It’s working.

Day 01July 2026
Campaign live

It’s live. Here’s what that actually means.

The Kickstarter is up. After months of scripts, scouting, and buying fake blood in quantities that raise eyebrows at the till — there’s a page with a button on it and a number that starts at zero.

Let’s be straight about what this is. We’re making this film either way. Our own money is already in it. The Kickstarter doesn’t decide whether Echoes of Crimson exists — it decides how far it travels, how much we can pay the people who turn up, and whether the version you eventually watch looks like what’s in our heads or a compromise we settled for.

On backing it

Tiers start at £5. From £25 you get both cuts — Eileen’s chaos comedy horror and Jason’s darker, colder one, the same footage taken into two rooms and coming out as two different films. Above that there’s signed artwork, DVDs, screen‑worn costume, and a handful of one‑of‑one props that when they’re gone are gone.

If you’re going to back it, back it early. We’re not saying that to rush you. Kickstarter’s algorithm surfaces campaigns that move fast in the first couple of days, and a project that stalls out of the gate rarely recovers no matter how good it is. A fiver on day one genuinely does more work than fifty quid in week three. That’s not sales talk, it’s just how the platform behaves.

And every backer name goes up on the Backer Wall, in the order it arrived. First in, top of the wall, and it stays that way for good.

On not backing it

Here’s the part we mean most. Not everyone has spare money, and we’re not going to pretend otherwise. Times are hard and a horror film is not anybody’s priority over a food shop. If you can’t pledge, that is completely fine, and you are not less welcome here for it.

Sharing is real support. It costs nothing and it works. Independent films don’t find their audience through advertising budgets — they find it because somebody tagged a mate who likes British horror. Post the link. Repost the poster. Tell one person who’d get it. Leave a comment on the campaign, because comments push a project up the page in a way that silence never will.

We have watched films we love disappear because nobody outside the room ever heard about them. If you spread this thing further than it would have gone on its own, you have done something for it. We’ll take that as gladly as a pledge, and we mean that plainly.

Where to start

If you want to know who’s making this before you decide anything — fair enough. The Watch page now has Eileen’s showreel, Jason’s showreel, and trailers for what the pair of us have made before. Thirty years of British horror between us and no interest in slowing down.

Then come back and press the button. Or don’t, and tell somebody instead. Both help.

Day 00July 2026
Pre‑production

Before a single frame

Nothing's been shot yet. We're writing this from the wrong side of it — scripts locked, locations scouted, fake blood ordered in a quantity that got a look at the till. August is when the cameras roll. This is us telling you before, not after.

The experiment: one shoot, one cast, one set of performances in the fog and cobbles of 1888 Whitechapel. Then the footage goes into two rooms and comes out as two films. Eileen cuts the comedy horror — chaos, teeth, jokes that shouldn't land and do. Jason cuts the dark one — slower, colder, the version that follows you home. Neither is the "real" cut. That's the point.

We're making it either way. Our own money's already down and we're not waiting for anyone's blessing. The Kickstarter decides how far it travels, nothing more. If you're going to back it, back it early — early is the only thing that actually moves the needle.
